# Elderwood Frontier — Black Hand Faction Lore

Status: CANONICAL FACTION PREMISE — 2026-11-22. Narrative direction; quests and scenes remain implementation work.

## Central creed
**No knowledge forbidden. No power beyond our reach. No gods above us.**

The Black Hand believes magic is mortals' greatest hope of freedom and salvation. All schools of magic—including necromancy, blood magic, dangerous rites, ancient or forgotten disciplines, and powers others prohibit—must be investigated, mastered, and acquired. To declare a kind of magic off-limits is, in their view, to surrender one possible path to liberation.

They believe kings, churches and other institutions have restricted the study of magic out of fear, ignorance, convenience, or desire to control others. They see no obligation to respect those boundaries. Their pursuit of magical knowledge is deliberately indiscriminate: leave no stone unturned and seize power wherever possible.

Their ultimate collective ambition is to gain the strength needed to overthrow **the gods who rule over mortals**. This ambition strengthens their determination to search every realm and pursue every kind of power. Treat the gods' nature, methods and the practical possibility of overthrow as questions for the larger story to explore, rather than pre-resolving all cosmology in the faction premise.

## Not a benevolent or misunderstood faction
The Black Hand is an **evil faction**. It is home to individuals who are cruel, sadistic, unstable, obsessive, exploitative, or excluded from conventional society and who welcome an organization that tolerates or rewards them. It contains genuinely dangerous people and commits harmful deeds. Its liberation rhetoric does not absolve its members' actions or turn it into a secretly good organization.

Yet members do **not** all share the same personal motives. Some genuinely seek liberation from all external authority. Others seek the strength to dominate or replace those now ruling. Others prize research and experimentation above human cost. Still others want license for cruelty and have found a place that welcomes their talents. These motives may overlap, and they create organic rivalries and hard choices.

## Internal ideological tensions
- **Liberators:** No being should command the freedom of another; dethrone the gods so no one has divine masters again.
- **Ascendants:** Wrest divine power away so the Black Hand—or its most powerful members—can rule in the gods' place.
- **Unrestricted scholars:** The duty to discover transcends ordinary prohibitions; no forbidden field may be abandoned.
- **Fanatics and destroyers:** Institutions must be broken regardless of the collateral damage.
- **Predators and outcasts:** The Black Hand offers access, protection or belonging to people unwilling or unable to live by ordinary social constraints.

These are useful perspectives for narrative writing, not five mandatory formal subfactions. Differences become consequential as members approach actual power. In particular, the pursuit of liberation can lead to a new tyranny: the power sought to free mortals may produce another set of masters.

## Connection to the awakening prophecy
The opening ruin's magic stone reads: *Magic is our hope. It has the power to set us free. As it awakens, the world will change.*

This resonates powerfully with the Black Hand's doctrine. Members disagree about whether the awakening will emancipate ordinary mortals, empower a chosen few, destabilize divine rule, or supply the weapons needed to overthrow the gods. Their appetite for exploring portals and new magical realms is a core narrative and gameplay motivation: each realm may contain knowledge, forgotten rituals, creatures, relics, or power that conventional institutions have never dared pursue.

## Player agency and mentorship
The Black Hand is a principal fully developed route in the first content production pass. Its five-year apprenticeship should feature an influential mentor, distinct quests, genuine power growth, forbidden schools and faction relationships. The player may act brutally, pragmatically, ambitiously or with genuine concern for freedom while still participating in an evil organization; decisions can have meaningful consequences. Do not turn the player automatically into a morally good hero just because they selected a liberator argument.

The church and lord's household are separate mentor choices and must remain structurally possible; fully authoring their faction questlines can follow completion of the Black Hand route. Keep the shared birthday rite, Adventurer's Guild and awakening ruin prologue common to all choices.

## Writing guidelines
- Give villains specific motives rather than making everyone identical.
- Preserve that the organization as a whole seeks unrestricted magical discovery and the overthrow of the gods, not merely accumulating generic power.
- Let members sincerely disagree about what freedom means and who benefits.
- Do not confuse Black Hand power, the existence of gods, or the awakening prophecy with proof that their interpretation is correct.
- Preserve existing routes into the Black Hand through the graveyard/grimoire or the cellar guard, and connect its politics to portal exploration, ruins and the wider world.
